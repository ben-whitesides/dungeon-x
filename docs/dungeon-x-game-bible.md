## 1.1 THE SUNSTONE MYTH — ORIGIN STORY

### The Age of Radiance

Three thousand years before the events of Dungeon X, the world was lit by a single artifact of impossible power: **The Sunstone**. Not a sun — a crystallized fragment of the first dawn, forged in the collision between the planes of Light and Shadow before either existed cleanly. The ancient order that discovered it called themselves **The Aureate Circle**, twelve scholars who believed the stone's power could sustain their civilization indefinitely.

They were right. For four centuries, the Sunstone powered the city of **Aldenmere** — healing crops, extending lives, lighting the underground warrens where their people worked. Aldenmere became the center of the known world, a city where darkness literally could not take hold.

### The Fracture

In Year 412 of the Aureate Age, a court mage named **Dorevus the Pale** argued that the Sunstone's power was being wasted on mere utility. He believed the stone could unmake death itself — that the barrier between the living and the dead was a door, and the Sunstone was its key.

The Circle refused. Dorevus performed the ritual alone.

He did not unmake death. He cracked the Sunstone.

The explosion that followed did not destroy Aldenmere. It scattered it. The twelve scholars of the Aureate Circle died in the initial burst. Dorevus was not killed — he was unmade. His body ceased to exist in the present, but his consciousness survived, anchored to the ten shards of the Sunstone as they flew across the world, each embedding itself in a different place — high mountain peaks, deep ocean caves, ancient ruins, volcanic chambers. Wherever a fragment landed, something changed. Light behaved differently. Old things woke up.

Aldenmere still exists, a ruin now, at the edge of maps. Where the Sunstone once sat, there is a scar of absolute darkness the size of a village square. Nothing enters it. Nothing returns.

### The Present Day

Three thousand years of mythology and forgetting have passed. The Sunstone is legend. Most scholars believe it never existed. The dungeons where fragments landed have been built up over, collapsed, flooded, or claimed by the creatures that gathered around each shard's ambient power.

The player arrives at **The Rusty Flagon** with nothing. They are not a hero of prophecy. They are an adventurer who heard a rumor, or lost a bet, or simply has nowhere else to go.

The barkeep, Aldric, doesn't believe in the Sunstone. The first NPC who mentions it (Mira the Cartographer) calls it a "useful fantasy for selling maps." Even as the player assembles fragments, the world does not celebrate. The tavern notices. Slowly. The whispers begin.

### The Villain — Dorevus the Unmoored

Dorevus is not a boss in the traditional sense. He is a presence.

His consciousness has spent three millennia drifting between the ten shards, unable to fully reconstitute without them being assembled in one place. He is the final threat, the reason no one who has ever found more than two fragments has returned to discuss it.

He is not evil by nature. He is a man who made one catastrophic mistake and has spent three thousand years trying to fix it, growing more desperate and less recognizably human with each century.

**His goal:** When all ten fragments are assembled, Dorevus can reconstitute. He believes if he reforms his body and repeats the ritual correctly this time, he can unmake death as he intended — and undo everything that happened when he cracked the stone. He wants to save the world by resetting it to the moment before he broke it.

The player is not a hero stopping an evil plan. They are the first person in three thousand years who has collected enough fragments that Dorevus is watching, waiting, and in the final dungeon, waiting for them.

**His influence:** Dorevus cannot act directly until the 10th dungeon. But each shard contains a fragment of his awareness. NPCs near the dungeons report strange dreams. Enemies near shards are subtly different — more organized, more purposeful. Wall markings occasionally appear that no adventurer wrote. These are Dorevus, reaching.

## 1.2 THE TAVERN — THE RUSTY FLAGON

**Location:** The village of **Thornhaven**, a settlement of roughly 300 people on the edge of the Aldenmere wastes. Far enough from civilization that the desperate and ambitious pass through. Close enough to the ruins that it has a history.

**The Building:** Two-story timber frame. Ground floor: common room, bar, fireplace, notice board, merchant corner. Trophy alcove near the hearth. Back door to a small stable yard. Upstairs: 4 rented rooms (party rests here between runs).

### Permanent NPCs

**Aldric Greaves — The Barkeep**
Male, 58. Heavyset. Missing two fingers on his left hand (dungeon accident, 30 years ago). He went once. He does not talk about it. He serves ale, gives rumors, and occasionally says things with an economy of words that suggest he knows more than he lets on. He reacts to the player's reputation. At Legendary Champion tier, he pours a drink without being asked.
- Rumors he gives: Current dungeon rumors, general world news, cryptic observations
- Hades mechanic: After enough deaths, he stops pretending he doesn't know what you're doing
- At 5 fragments found: "You know they say the last person who found three never came back. You found five."

**Mira — The Cartographer**
Female, 34. Covered in ink stains. Obsessively draws maps that don't match any territory anyone else recognizes. She will sell the player partial dungeon maps for gold. Her maps are always right — but they show things that haven't happened yet.
- Provides: Map fragments for sale, dungeon entrance locations, overworld clues
- Character arc: Increasingly unnerved as the player collects fragments. By fragment 8, she has drawn the final dungeon layout without knowing why.

**Orin Vane — The Wounded Sellsword**
Male, 27. Permanent limp. Loud laugh. He went into Dungeon 2 (Goblin Warrens) looking for a missing sibling. He did not find his sibling. He will not go back. He tells combat stories that are partly true. He can teach the player about enemy behavior patterns.
- Provides: Enemy intel hints, combat tips disguised as war stories, reputation reactions
- Character arc: At enough reputation, he asks you to bring back proof of what happened to his sibling. The answer is in Dungeon 2, Floor 3.

**The Ghost at Table Seven — "Elden"**
An apparition that only appears after the player's first dungeon death. He sits at Table Seven — a corner table, slightly too shadowed. He was a famous adventurer three hundred years ago. He watches. Occasionally tips his cup. He gives cryptic observations that are mechanically useful.
- Provides: Hades-style narrative commentary after deaths — "That's the third time I've watched someone die to the Frost Wraith. Same way each time."
- At fragment 7: "I found three, once. Something came out of the walls after the third one. Something wearing my face."

**Bessa — The Merchant**
Female, 44. Runs the supply corner with pragmatic energy. Sells torches, food, potions, arrows, scrolls. Prices react to player reputation (CHA matters here). She knows product quality — calls out fake potions and bad arrows, which is how players learn to trust her.
- Provides: All consumables, identified items, occasional rare stock (changes daily with seed)

**The Notice Board**
Physical object in the tavern. Shows 2-3 dungeon options with: skull rating (1-5), fragment name, rumored treasure, cryptic lore entry. Daily seed determines which dungeons appear. Some dungeons only appear when the player has found N fragments prior.

## Dungeon Difficulty Scale
- **Tier I (Dungeons 1-3):** Introductory. 1-2 floors. 3-5 enemy types. Puzzle complexity: single-step.
- **Tier II (Dungeons 4-6):** Intermediate. 3 floors. 6-8 enemy types. Puzzle complexity: two-step chains.
- **Tier III (Dungeons 7-9):** Advanced. 4 floors. 8-10 enemy types. Puzzle complexity: multi-element combinations.
- **Tier IV (Dungeon 10):** The Final Descent. 5 floors. All enemy types + Dorevus encounter.

## DUNGEON 1 — THE WHISPERING CRYPTS

**Theme:** Undead burial ground. Dusty crypts, crumbling sarcophagi, dripping water.
**Environment:** Stone dungeon. Gray walls with purple-black shadows. Cracked ceiling tiles. Ancient murals half-visible under grime. Occasional bones on the floor. Torch sconces already mounted but unlit.
**Fragment:** Fragment of Dawn (always the first found, located behind a sealed sarcophagus door)
**Fragment Lore:** "The first light that ever fell on this world. It warms even now, faintly, behind ancient stone."

**Floor Count:** 2
**Difficulty Tier:** I (Tutorial dungeon)

**Unique Mechanic — Sarcophagus Drag:**
Sarcophagus lids can be dragged (push mechanic). Some contain loot (gold, potions, old weapons). Some contain enemies that wake up when disturbed. Players learn drag-and-drop here in a relatively safe context. Tutorial integration point.

**Enemy Roster:**
1. **Crypt Crawler** — AC 10, HP 12, Attack: Bite d4, Vulnerability: Fire, XP: 50
2. **Shadow Lurker** (already built) — AC 12, HP 28
3. **Bone Revenant** (already built) — AC 16, HP 52 (Floor 2 only)

**Mini-Boss (Floor 2):** **The Vault Warden** — Ancient skeleton in ceremonial armor. AC 17, HP 65, Attack: Ceremonial Halberd +5, d10+3. Has a shield that must be destroyed first (200 HP shield, fire vulnerability). When shield destroyed, Warden enters Enraged mode (+2 attacks per turn). Drops: Iron Key, 40 gold, chance of +1 long sword.

**Boss:** None for Floor 2. Fragment is guarded by the sealed sarcophagus puzzle (fire+stone combo required to open).

**Puzzle:** Sealed door (fire+stone combo). Tutorial-level hint: visible runes above door show fire and stone symbols. Wall marking: "The Aureate Circle sealed this with their craft. Heat and weight as one." This introduces the spell combo system explicitly.

**Loot Tier:** Common. Iron key, basic armor, health potions, 20-50 gold per run.

**Overworld Path:** Graveyard walk. Crumbling stone walls, dead trees, raven perches. 30 seconds. Side cave: Collapsed mausoleum. 1 room, 2 enemies, guaranteed 1 potion.

## DUNGEON 2 — THE GOBLIN WARRENS

**Theme:** Chaotic goblin civilization. Improvised construction on top of older ruins. Loud, cluttered, surprisingly intelligent enemies.
**Environment:** Low ceilings (5 feet — humans must crouch, shown in HUD indicator). Rope bridges over pit gaps. Cooking fires with actual food. Graffiti on walls in broken common tongue. Stolen gear hanging from hooks.
**Fragment:** Fragment of Dusk — "Found in the den of small, fierce things who mistook it for a pretty rock."
**Fragment Lore:** "The last light before night. Patient. It has waited here for centuries without complaint."

**Floor Count:** 2
**Difficulty Tier:** I

**Unique Mechanic — Pit Bridges:**
Rope bridges cross 2-tile-wide pits. Goblins can cut bridges (if they spot player, 20% chance per goblin turn). Player can cut bridges too (knife on rope = drag interaction) to trap pursuing enemies in pit. Goblins who fall in a pit are not dead — they respawn at the ladder on the far side in 3 turns unless the player continues moving.

**Enemy Roster:**
1. **Goblin Scrapper** — AC 12, HP 10, Attack: Rusty Dagger d4, Fast (acts twice per turn), XP: 40
2. **Goblin Archer** — AC 11, HP 8, Attack: Short Bow d6 (ranged, 3-tile range), XP: 40
3. **Goblin Shaman** — AC 11, HP 18, Attack: Shadow Bolt d6, can Curse (reduces AC by 2), XP: 80
4. **War Goblin** — AC 14, HP 35, Attack: Cleaver d8+2, Has morale mechanic (flees at 25% HP), XP: 120

**Mini-Boss (Floor 1):** **The Chief's Favorite** — Goblin champion. AC 15, HP 45. Has stolen plate gauntlets (+1 to attack). Shouts taunts each turn that reveal goblin behavior patterns as hints. Drops: Gold Key (needed for Floor 2 main room), 30 gold.

**Boss (Floor 2):** **Gretchka the Elder** — Old goblin shaman. Enormous for a goblin (4 feet tall). AC 13, HP 80. Attacks: Shadow Bolt d8, Mass Curse (all party -1 AC, WIS save DC 14 to resist), Summon Scrapper (calls 2 goblins per turn if any goblin dens are left standing). Mechanic: 3 goblin dens on the floor. Destroy all dens first = she can't summon. Fight her with dens up = infinite enemies. Drops: Sunstone Fragment of Dusk, Shaman's Amulet (+2 WIS), 60 gold.

**Orin Vane's Quest:** His sibling's journal is in Floor 3 (inaccessible in Tier I — revisit in Phase 3). Wall marking in Floor 2: "Joren Vane. Floor 3. He didn't make it out." This plants the quest hook for Orin's sidequest in Phase 3.

**Puzzle:** The Chief's Treasure Room requires shooting an arrow through a narrow slot (Ranger specialty). Other classes can use an Arrow Scroll (consumable) or find a workaround — a breakable grate (Fighter), or a bypass tunnel (search check, WIS).

**Loot Tier:** Common + uncommon. Stolen gear (random quality), gold 30-70, chance of goblin-crafted items (unusual stats, e.g., Crude Helm: AC +2 but -1 CHA because it smells awful).

## DUNGEON 3 — THE FLOODED VAULTS

**Theme:** Ancient treasure vault, half-submerged by a broken aqueduct. Underwater sections, floating debris, rusted iron doors.
**Environment:** First-person view: lower third of viewport shows water surface level. Stone walls streaked with mineral deposits (blue-green). Bubbles rising. Distant sound of rushing water. Some rooms fully submerged (hold breath mechanic).
**Fragment:** Fragment of Storm — "It crackles with static even underwater. The fish avoid it."
**Fragment Lore:** "The breath of the sky, compressed into stone. It has not forgotten where it came from."

**Floor Count:** 2
**Difficulty Tier:** I — transitional (introduces breath mechanic)

**Unique Mechanic — Submersion & Breath:**
Some corridors are fully submerged. Player can hold breath for 20 seconds (shown as a bar). Moving costs breath faster than standing. Reaching 0: HP drain at 5/second. Torches go out underwater (must relight after surfacing). Enemies underwater: some use this space advantageously (Drowned), some hate it (undead constructs, fire-based enemies).

**Enemy Roster:**
1. **Vault Rat** — AC 10, HP 8, Bite d3, Swarms (3 attack as one), XP: 30
2. **Drowned** — AC 12, HP 22, Waterlogged undead, Attack: Grapple (holds player, no other actions until broken, STR contest DC 13), Vulnerability: Fire, Full immunity to cold, XP: 90
3. **Iron Golem Sentry** — AC 18, HP 55, Slam d10+4, Immunity: Fire, Shadow, Poison. Only magic weapons or Stone spells do full damage. Slow (acts every other turn). XP: 200
4. **Treasure Mimic** — AC 14, HP 40, disguised as chest. On open attempt: ambush (free first attack d8+3). If identified before opening (WIS check DC 15, or Sense Evil Cleric ability): can be fought normally. XP: 180

**Boss (Floor 2):** **The Vault Keeper** — An Iron Golem that was reactivated when the water rose. AC 19, HP 120. Attacks: Slam d12+5, Flood Release (opens a valve, raising water level — player has 3 turns to reach high ground or take drowning damage). Special: Cannot be damaged by non-magical attacks. Stone spell + Wind spell = Sonic Fracture (combo, bypasses immunity). Drops: Fragment of Storm, Vault Master's Ring (+1 INT), 80 gold.

**Puzzle:** Aqueduct control room. Three valves in specific order drain different sections of the dungeon. Wrong order floods rooms. Correct order: read the carved instruction plate in the first room (Mage can decipher runes; Ranger can spot the order marked on the valves; Fighter can trial-and-error if they accept flooding; Cleric can divine the sequence with WIS check DC 12).

**Loot Tier:** Common + uncommon. Waterproof containers (useful meta-item), underwater breathing amulet (uncommon), gold 50-90, rusted (cheaper) versions of mid-tier armor.

## DUNGEON 4 — EMBER DEPTHS

**Theme:** Volcanic caverns. Active lava flows. Heat as a survival resource (too hot without protection = HP drain).
**Environment:** Red-orange lighting dominates. Cracked rock floors with glowing lava below. Steam vents. Fire-resistant doors. Some floors made of cooled lava stone that can crack under heavy footsteps (Fighter heavy armor = floor warnings).
**Fragment:** Fragment of Flame — "It is warm enough to cook meat. Adventurers have tried. Don't."
**Fragment Lore:** "The first fire was not made by humans. This piece remembers that."

**Floor Count:** 3
**Difficulty Tier:** II

**Unique Mechanic — Heat Exposure:**
Heat Bar replaces the standard torch anxiety here. Without fire protection gear (Fire Resistance Cloak, Ice Shield amulet, Ranger's Heat Ward ability), the Heat Bar rises 1%/10 seconds. At 50%: -1 STR (armor is heavy, sweating). At 75%: -2 STR, -1 DEX. At 100%: HP drain 3/second. Ice spells temporarily reduce Heat Bar. Standing near a lava vent: +10% instantly. Fire Resistance gear from Dungeon 3 loot or Bessa's shop becomes relevant here.

**Enemy Roster:**
1. **Magma Spawn** — AC 11, HP 20, Lava Touch d6 fire, Immunity: Fire, Vulnerability: Ice, XP: 70
2. **Ember Hound** — AC 13, HP 35, Bite d8 fire, Fast (acts twice), Burns on death (1d4 fire damage to adjacent targets), XP: 130
3. **Flame Salamander** — AC 14, HP 48, Fire Breath (cone, 3d4 fire, DEX save DC 14 half), Ranged, stays 2 tiles away, XP: 180
4. **Stone Drake** — AC 17, HP 75, Claw d10+3, Immunity: Fire and Stone, Tail Sweep (hits all adjacent, DEX save DC 15), XP: 280
5. **Ash Revenant** — AC 13, HP 40, Ash Cloud (blinds for 2 turns, CON save DC 13), undead variant, Vulnerability: Wind (disperses ash), XP: 150

**Mini-Boss (Floor 2):** **The Furnace Warden** — Fire construct, living armor, AC 16, HP 90. Immune to fire. Shadow strike and Ice attacks deal double. If player uses Ice spell in combat: causes instant steam cloud — reduces visibility (torchlight radius halved for 3 turns), enemy confused (50% miss chance for 1 turn). Drops: Ember Key (Floor 3 access), Fire Resistance Cloak, 50 gold.

**Boss (Floor 3):** **Ignis the Sleeping Wyrm** — Not fully awakened. Semi-intelligent. AC 18, HP 180. Attacks: Inferno Breath (60-degree cone, 4d6 fire, DEX save DC 16), Lava Slam (d12+6, shakes floor — all standing targets STR save DC 14 or knocked prone, -2 AC until stood up), Tail Crush (1d10+5, can hit back row). Special: Lava Pool mechanic — on Ignis taking 50 damage, he opens a vent. A lava pool expands 1 tile per turn from one corner. Players must finish the fight before the pool reaches them. Wind+Stone combo: Seal Vent (closes the pool). Drops: Fragment of Flame, Dragon Scale (crafting material — makes Dragon Scale Armor), 120 gold.

**Puzzle Floor 2:** A collapsed bridge over a lava rift. Wind+Stone = sonic column (construct a stone pillar using the air pressure). Alternatively: Fighter can throw a stone block to extend a partial platform (STR DC 15). Ranger can shoot a rope arrow to a hook on the far wall.

**Loot Tier:** Uncommon + rare. Fire-resistant gear becomes available. Fire arrows (ranged enhancement). Volcanic crystal (crafting). Gold 80-150.

## DUNGEON 5 — THE FROZEN ABYSS

**Theme:** Arctic underground. A glacial cavern system beneath a mountain. Ice floors, frozen enemies, ancient things locked in ice for centuries.
**Environment:** Cool blue-white lighting. Ice walls (semi-transparent — shapes visible through them). Frozen breath visible as player moves. Slippery floor mechanic (DEX save to avoid sliding). Things frozen inside walls — some dead, some not.
**Fragment:** Fragment of Frost — "It dropped the temperature of the vault by four degrees the moment the Ranger picked it up."
**Fragment Lore:** "The cold does not hate. It simply does not care. This is the piece that was there when the world first exhaled."

**Floor Count:** 3
**Difficulty Tier:** II

**Unique Mechanic — Ice Floors & Frozen Prisoners:**
Ice floors: Moving fast on ice requires DEX save DC 10 or slide 1-2 tiles in direction traveled (into walls, enemies, or traps). Solving: Equip Spiked Boots (inventory item, medium weight) for immunity to sliding. Frozen Prisoners: Some rooms have creatures frozen in walls. Approach with fire spell = they thaw. Some are allies (reward), some are enemies (fight), some are traps (they grab you as they thaw, restrained until STR contest). Discovery model — player must decide to thaw each one.

**Enemy Roster:**
1. **Ice Sprite** — AC 13, HP 15, Frost Bolt d6 cold, Incorporeal (50% miss physical), XP: 80
2. **Frost Wraith** (already built) — AC 13, HP 22
3. **Glacial Bear** — AC 14, HP 65, Mauling Swipe d10+4, can Freeze the ground (Ice spell from body), XP: 220
4. **Rime Lich** — AC 15, HP 55, Cold Drain d8 (also drains 1 STR until rest), Raise Ice Dead (calls 2 frozen enemies back as Ice Spawn), XP: 240
5. **Avalanche Titan** — AC 16, HP 100, Stomp d12 (slide all adjacent 2 tiles), Snow Blind (WIS save DC 15 or blinded 2 turns), XP: 350

**Boss (Floor 3):** **The Warden of the Abyss** — A Rime Lich of enormous power. Has been here since the Sunstone fractured. AC 17, HP 200. Attacks: Cold Drain d10 (STR drain component), Ice Prison (one target immobilized 3 turns, CON save DC 16 to resist), Blizzard (AOE, everyone takes 3d6 cold, DEX save DC 15 half), Raise the Fallen (at 100 HP, reanimates the corpses of all defeated enemies in the room). Special: Fire+Wind = Inferno Gust (deals double and melts any ice walls in the room, opening new pathways for escape). Second phase at 50 HP: The Warden merges with the ice floor — can only be targeted by AOE spells or fire. Drops: Fragment of Frost, Warden's Staff (+2 INT, +1 to all cold spell damage), 160 gold.

**Frozen Prisoner Event (Floor 2):** One room contains a human frozen in the wall — wearing Aureate Circle robes. Thawing him reveals Cassius Aldenmere, a descendant of one of the twelve original scholars. He gives: history of the Sunstone not available anywhere else, location hint for Dungeon 7, and a Sigil of the Circle (consumable — dispels any magic door in any dungeon). He cannot leave — his body has deteriorated too much. He asks the player to complete what the Circle started.

**Loot Tier:** Uncommon + rare. Cold resistance gear, Frostbite arrows (apply Frost status), Rime Crystal (crafting material). Gold 100-180.

## DUNGEON 6 — THE SHATTERED HALLS

**Theme:** Ancient dwarven engineering complex, destroyed in an earthquake (caused by the Sunstone fracture). Half of every room is rubble. Mechanisms still function but unpredictably.
**Environment:** Gray stone with orange iron mechanisms — gears, pulleys, counterweights. Half-collapsed ceilings. Rubble piles that can be cleared (Fighter) or climbed. Trap density highest of any dungeon so far.
**Fragment:** Fragment of Stone — "It is impossibly heavy. A Ranger once found one and could not lift it. A Mage solved the problem with Wind."
**Fragment Lore:** "The earth does not break easily. This piece is all that broke."

**Floor Count:** 3
**Difficulty Tier:** II

**Unique Mechanic — Mechanism Interaction:**
Large mechanical levers, counterweights, and gear systems are the primary puzzle interface. They don't break when pulled — they chain-react. Example: Pull Lever A → counterweight drops → stone door opens (Floor 2 access). But the dropped counterweight also breaks a water pipe → Floor 1 slowly floods (10 min timer). Players learn to consider second-order effects before pulling anything.

**Enemy Roster:**
1. **Rubble Golem** — AC 15, HP 55, Slam d8+3, Immunity: Stone and Wind, Half damage from physical, Vulnerability: Ice (shatters stone bonds), XP: 190
2. **Dwarven Construct** — AC 16, HP 70, Steam Blast d10 (CON save DC 14 or Stunned 1 turn), Stone immunity, Can repair itself (15 HP per 2 turns if not attacked), XP: 280
3. **Gear Spider** — AC 13, HP 25, Grapple Web (Restrained, STR save DC 13 to break free), Fast (acts twice), Vulnerability: Fire (their webbing burns), XP: 100
4. **Cave Troll** — AC 14, HP 85, Club d12+5, Regenerate (5 HP/turn), Only stops regenerating with fire or acid damage, Rock Throw (ranged, d10, 4-tile range), XP: 300
5. **Shard Horror** — AC 12, HP 35, Flying crystal shards d6 (hits all targets in a line, DEX save DC 13 half), Vulnerability: Shadow (confuses targeting), XP: 140

**Boss (Floor 3):** **The Forgemaster's Legacy** — A dwarven engineer who fused himself with his own machinery to survive the Sunstone fracture. Three thousand years of running on steam power has not improved his disposition. AC 18, HP 220. Phase 1: Uses steam vents (AOE d8 fire to 3 tiles), Gear Crush (d12+6 to one target), Barricade (creates a wall of debris, blocks movement). Phase 2 at 110 HP: Merges fully with the forge. Immobile but fires pistons in sequence — telegraph shows which tiles are unsafe next turn. Must disable 4 steam valves around the room to unlock final phase. Phase 3: Vulnerable, AC drops to 14, but Enraged — acts twice per turn. Drops: Fragment of Stone, Forgemaster's Gauntlets (+2 STR, can break walls with bare hands), 200 gold.

**Puzzle — The Sequence Lock:** Floor 2 main gate uses a 6-mechanism sequence. Clues are on the walls (Mage reads runes, Ranger finds the engraved sequence on a gear surface, Fighter brute-forces and hits the right sequence by accident on a 1-in-6 random check, Cleric senses the divine resonance of which levers have been touched most). This is the most puzzle-heavy dungeon — designed to reward the party composition that has all classes.

**Loot Tier:** Rare + legendary uncommon. Dwarven Craft weapons (d8+1 base, no condition degrades), gear-made armor (+1 AC baseline), Gear Spider webbing (crafting material for Ranger traps). Gold 130-200.

## DUNGEON 7 — THE HOWLING SPIRE

**Theme:** A collapsed wizard's tower, the upper floors now open to the sky on a mountain peak. Vertical dungeon — player moves up, not down. Wind is a constant environmental force.
**Environment:** Brown stone lower floors transitioning to open-air sections at the top. Wind particles visible at edges of the viewport. Some platforms exposed — falling is possible. Sky visible in the first-person view as ceilings collapse into open air. Day/night cycle visible (changes enemy types at certain points).
**Fragment:** Fragment of Wind — "It rattled in the chest so violently the Ranger who found it thought something was alive inside."
**Fragment Lore:** "Wind does not follow the same rules as the other elements. It was the first to leave the Sunstone and the last to slow down."

**Floor Count:** 4
**Difficulty Tier:** III

**Unique Mechanic — Wind Currents & Falling:**
Wind direction indicator in HUD corner. Moving against strong wind costs double movement. Being knocked back by an attack on wind-exposed floors: additional DEX save (DC 14) or pushed 2 tiles toward the edge. Falling from an open floor: 2d6 fall damage per floor fallen, land on the floor below. Ranger can negate fall damage (Tumble ability). Wind also affects ranged attacks: shooting into wind = disadvantage on ranged attacks. Shooting with wind = advantage. Magic: Wind spells become double power here but twice the cost.

**Enemy Roster:**
1. **Storm Raptor** — AC 14, HP 40, Talon Strike d8+2, Dive Bomb (from ceiling, d10, no attack roll required, lands on target tile — telegraph 1 turn ahead), XP: 160
2. **Wind Shade** — AC 13, HP 30, Incorporeal, Wind Scream (all targets, d6, DEX save DC 13 or pushed 1 tile), Vulnerability: Stone (anchor it), XP: 130
3. **Gargoyle** — AC 17, HP 65, Petrifying Gaze (WIS save DC 15 or Paralyzed 2 turns), Stone immunity, Flying, Ranged attacks at disadvantage against it, XP: 250
4. **Spire Wraith** — AC 14, HP 50, Thunder Strike d10 lightning, Chain Lightning (hits adjacent targets for half damage on failed DEX save DC 14), XP: 220
5. **Ancient Sentinel** — AC 19, HP 100, +7 to hit, d10+5, was the tower's guardian. Vulnerability: None. Weakness: Cannot move while player stands still (built to attack moving targets), XP: 400

**Boss (Floor 4 — Open Air Summit):** **The Wind's Voice** — Not a creature — the fragment's accumulated energy has taken a form. A tornado column, semi-intelligent, remembering Dorevus's touch. AC 15 (hard to hit a tornado), HP 250. Has no body. Attacks: Cyclone (all targets, 3d6, DEX save DC 16), Wind Lance (single target, d12 lightning), Updraft (lifts one target, helpless for 2 turns then dropped for fall damage). Cannot be harmed by physical or stone attacks. Wind spells heal it. Ice spells slow it (AC drops to 11 for 2 turns). Shadow+Light = Reveal true form: underneath the tornado is a crystalline core. Core can be physically attacked. Destroying the core ends the fight. Drops: Fragment of Wind, Gale Mantle (Armor: +1 AC, immune to pushed/knockback effects), 220 gold.

**Vertical Puzzle — The Bell Tower:** Floor 3 is a bell tower. Three massive bells must ring in the correct sequence (determined by tone — Mage recognizes musical notation on the wall, others can trial-and-error from 6 possible sequences). Correct sequence opens a hidden room with a Sigil of the Circle. Wrong sequence: triggers a Gargoyle ambush (2 Gargoyles drop from the ceiling).

**Loot Tier:** Rare. Wind-enhanced gear (speed boosts, falling immunity), Storm Arrow (lightning arrow, chain damage), Cloud Crystal (crafting). Gold 160-250.

---

## DUNGEON 8 — THE VOID CHAMBERS

**Theme:** Sub-dimensional space beneath an old cult headquarters. Reality is unstable here. Walls shift. Some rooms exist in two states simultaneously. Shadow creatures evolved over millennia in the presence of the Sunstone's dark fragment.
**Environment:** Black stone walls. Deep purple ambient light — no torches needed (light source replaced by ambient Void glow, but enemies benefit from it too). Some walls are translucent in places (see-through — things seen in adjacent rooms but unreachable). Gravity anomalies in certain rooms (ceiling becomes floor for 1 turn, then switches back).
**Fragment:** Fragment of Shadow — "The player who found it reported that it cast a shadow in a different direction than they were standing."
**Fragment Lore:** "Shadow is not the absence of light. The Sunstone knew this. This piece is the proof."

**Floor Count:** 4
**Difficulty Tier:** III

**Unique Mechanic — Shadow Phase:**
Shadow creatures have a Phase state — between turns, they exist partially in the Void. In Phase: immune to physical attacks, only Shadow or Light spells land. At start of their turn: Phase ends, physical attacks resume. HUD indicator shows Phase status. Player can learn timing — attack before they Phase, use spells during Phase. Player can also enter a Shadow Phase themselves using the Fragment of Shadow (once collected): 10 seconds of Phase, immune to physical attacks, can move through thin walls, cannot attack while Phased.

**Enemy Roster:**
1. **Void Tendril** — AC 11, HP 20, Lash d6 shadow, Phases every other turn, Vulnerability: Light, XP: 90
2. **Shadow Clone** — AC 13, HP 35, identical to player's class in appearance, Mirrors player's last action back at them (if player attacked for 8, Clone attacks for 8), XP: 160
3. **Null Hound** — AC 15, HP 55, Void Bite d10+2, Reality Distortion (room map briefly corrupts — player must navigate by memory for 2 turns), XP: 230
4. **Rift Walker** — AC 14, HP 70, Teleports 2 tiles before attacking, Flicker Strike d12 (can't miss — bypasses AC entirely, auto-hit), Can only be stunned (not dodged), XP: 320
5. **Void Titan** — AC 16, HP 120, Reality Crush d12+6, Timeline Split (duplicates self — one duplicate deals real damage, one is an illusion, WIS save DC 16 to identify the real one), XP: 500

**Boss (Floor 4):** **The Archivist of Nothing** — A cultist who achieved what Dorevus failed at: partial transcendence. Half in the physical world, half in the Void. AC 16, HP 280. Phase 1: Standard combat — Dark Bolt d10, Void Field (AOE, all targets in a 3x3 area, d8 shadow), Reality Unravel (remove one item from player's inventory into the Void — it's gone unless recovered). Phase 2 at 140 HP: Enters Full Phase — immune to physical, only Light/Shadow affects it. Shadow+Light combo (Reveal) forces it back into physical form permanently. Phase 3: Physical but enraged, all attacks deal +50%, but AC drops to 13. Drops: Fragment of Shadow, Void Mantle (+2 DEX, Shadow Phase ability for non-Mage classes), 280 gold.

**Puzzle — The Twin Mirrors:** Two large mirrors in a room. Objects placed in front of Mirror A appear in front of Mirror B and vice versa. Key required for Floor 4 exists only as a reflection in Mirror B — player must physically bring the actual key to Mirror A to "manifest" the reflection into reality. This requires understanding the mechanic (Mage gets an INT hint, others must experiment). This is the most abstract puzzle in the game.

**Loot Tier:** Rare + legendary. Void-touched gear (shadow resistance, Phase mechanic items), Null Arrow (bypasses AC like Rift Walker), Void Crystal (crafting). Gold 200-300.

---

## DUNGEON 9 — THE CRYSTAL SANCTUM

**Theme:** Ancient Aureate Circle headquarters. The most beautiful dungeon in the game — and the most dangerous. Light as a weapon. Perfect order maintained by magical automata that have been running for 3000 years.
**Environment:** White and gold stone. Light crystal chandeliers. Clean floors (the automata maintain them). Sacred geometry in wall carvings. Doors that judge worthiness. The only dungeon where enemies are not aggressive by default — they attack only if the player attacks first or breaks rules (looting certain items, entering restricted areas).
**Fragment:** Fragment of Light — "The last fragment before the final one. It outshines every other fragment held next to it. Mira's hands shook when she drew it on the map."
**Fragment Lore:** "The Circle put this one here on purpose. This is where they thought the world would be safest."

**Floor Count:** 4
**Difficulty Tier:** III

**Unique Mechanic — The Judgment System:**
Each major room has a Judgment threshold. Player accumulates Judgment points for: looting locked items (5 pts), attacking neutral automata (10 pts), breaking wards (15 pts), failing divine tests (5 pts). At 25 pts: Warning — automata become hostile. At 50 pts: Lockdown — all exits sealed, all automata hostile, Judgment Construct spawns (boss-tier enemy). At 100 pts: Banishment — player exits the dungeon involuntarily, no loot, run ends. CHA score reduces Judgment gain by 10% per 2 points above 12. Cleric has passive Sanctum Affinity: -20% Judgment gain.

**Enemy Roster (all hostile after Judgment threshold or from Floor 3 onward):**
1. **Light Golem** — AC 17, HP 70, Holy Strike d8+3 (counts as magic), Vulnerability: Shadow, XP: 280
2. **Aureate Sentinel** — AC 18, HP 90, Divine Chains (Restrained, STR save DC 16), Holy Volley (3 ranged strikes per turn, d6 each), XP: 350
3. **Judgment Construct** — AC 19, HP 130, Inescapable (always acts before player regardless of initiative), Edict (removes one ability from target for 3 turns — STR, DEX, etc. random), XP: 500
4. **Radiant Horror** — AC 15, HP 80, Blinding Flash (WIS save DC 16 or blinded 3 turns), Light Surge (room fills with light — shadow-type abilities fail for 2 turns), XP: 340
5. **The Restored** — AC 16, HP 100, Former adventurer resurrected by the Circle's systems. Uses a randomized weapon (adapts to player's class). Has WIS save proficiency (+6). XP: 420

**Boss (Floor 4):** **The Last Archivist** — The surviving consciousness of one of the twelve Aureate Circle scholars, preserved in a construct body for 3000 years. He is not evil. He is tired. He knows what the player is doing and why. He will offer to give them the Fragment without a fight — if they can answer three questions correctly (lore tests from journals and wall markings found throughout all 9 dungeons). Wrong answers: combat begins. Correct answers: fragment given freely, plus the Scholar's Codex (a meta-item that reveals the true nature of Dorevus and what happens in Dungeon 10). Combat if triggered: AC 20, HP 300, all attack types available, near-impossible without correct preparations. Drops: Fragment of Light, Scholar's Codex, Scholar's Ring (+3 to all spellcasting), 350 gold.

**The Three Questions (examples, rotated by seed):**
1. "What did Dorevus seek to unmake?" (Answer: Death)
2. "How many scholars died in the Fracture?" (Answer: Twelve)
3. "What is the name of the city that once stood where silence now lives?" (Answer: Aldenmere)

**Loot Tier:** Legendary. Aureate gear (highest tier, unique effects), Scholar items (intelligence bonuses), Light Crystal (crafting — only material for Holy weapons). Gold 300-400.

---

## DUNGEON 10 — THE FINAL DESCENT

**Theme:** The ruins of Aldenmere itself. The city is still standing, partially. The darkness at its center — where the Sunstone sat — has expanded. Dorevus has been waiting here.
**Environment:** Mix of all previous environments — stone, ice, shadow, void, light — all coexisting chaotically. The dungeon generates differently based on which class the player chose (Fighter: more combat corridors, Mage: more spell puzzle rooms, Cleric: more divine challenge rooms, Ranger: more trap/navigation rooms). 5 floors. Final floor is the Sunstone Scar — the location of the original fracture.
**Fragment:** Fragment of Life — "This one was not lost. Dorevus kept it. He is standing in front of it."
**Fragment Lore:** "Life did not need a name until the Sunstone broke. Now it does."

**Floor Count:** 5
**Difficulty Tier:** IV (Final)

**Unique Mechanic — Echo Chambers:**
Every 15 turns, an Echo Chamber activates — a room fills with echoes of the player's most common action (if they cast spells most, a spell-based trap; if they melee, melee ghosts of their previous attacks). The dungeon is learning the player's playstyle in real-time and countering it. This encourages variety and prevents one-trick approaches in the final dungeon.

**Enemy Roster — All Previous Types + Dungeon 10 Exclusives:**
1. **Aldenmere Shade** — Echoes of original citizens. AC 14, HP 50, Grief Touch (d8 shadow, also drains 1 WIS), XP: 200
2. **The Unmoored** — Creatures that Dorevus has touched directly. AC 16, HP 80, Reality Fracture (splits into 2 copies for 2 turns, only one is real), Dorevus's Mark (homing attack that finds the player regardless of tile, d10), XP: 380
3. **Timeline Fragment** — Boss-tier mini-enemy. AC 17, HP 100, is a copy of the player from a previous run (if ghost data exists) with their exact stats. XP: 500

**Boss (Floors 1-4 each have a Dungeon Warden):** Escalating difficulty — each floor's warden is drawn from the roster of previous dungeon bosses but enhanced. Floor 1 Warden: Vault Warden Enhanced (+50 HP, gains Shadow Phase). Floor 2: Gretchka Reborn (absorbed void energy, can summon Shadow Goblins). Floor 3: The Forgemaster's Ghost (incorporeal form, must Reveal to make physical). Floor 4: The Wind's Voice + Archivist merged form.

**Final Boss — Dorevus the Unmoored:**
Floor 5 — The Sunstone Scar.

The room: Circular chamber. The scar of darkness is in the center, 3x3 tiles of absolute black. Nine fragments the player has collected activate and float around the room. Dorevus stands at the far end, holding the Fragment of Life. He speaks first — a long monologue. He offers the player a choice:

- **Give him the fragments:** He attempts his ritual. Non-combat ending — ends with a cutscene. The world resets but differently. Dorevus succeeds, partially. The Sunstone mends. But Dorevus was not wrong — the world is changed. Not better or worse: different. Aldenmere is restored. The player is forgotten by the new timeline. Tavern epilogue: The Rusty Flagon exists but the player's reputation is gone. All NPCs are strangers.

- **Fight him:** The canonical combat path.

**Combat Stats:**
Phase 1: AC 18, HP 350. Attacks: Void Bolt d12, Time Slip (randomizes initiative order each turn), Fragment Pulse (one of the nine floating fragments is consumed per turn, each one applies a random elemental effect to the room — fire damage, ice floors, darkness, etc.).

Phase 2 at 175 HP: Dorevus merges with the Scar. The room becomes partially void-space. Immune to non-magical physical attacks. Uses all elemental types simultaneously. Timeline Fracture (all combatants roll initiative again, whoever rolled lowest must save DEX DC 18 or skip their next turn). Must combine Shadow+Light (Reveal) to force him back into physical form for 3 turns — maximum damage window.

Phase 3 at 75 HP: He disintegrates partially. Every attack has a 30% chance to pass through him (misses despite hitting AC). But his own attacks also have a 30% chance to fizzle. He is losing coherence. Combat ends when HP = 0 or if the player uses the Scholar's Codex item mid-combat (if they got it from Dungeon 9) — the Codex contains the original ritual. Using it here restores Dorevus's coherence momentarily, giving him a 5-second window of vulnerability (all attacks auto-crit), then he disperses peacefully.

**Victory Ending:**
The ten fragments assemble. The Sunstone mends — not perfect, but whole enough. Dorevus's consciousness disperses. Not death. Resolution. The darkness in the Scar recedes slightly. Aldenmere does not come back. The world does not change dramatically. But the tavern regulars notice something: the Fragment of Dawn on the trophy wall glows brighter. The ghost at Table Seven nods once and is not there the next morning.

The notice board now shows a new option: "Second Season." New artifact. New dungeons. The game continues.

# SECTION 3: FOUR CHARACTER CLASSES — FULL SPEC

---

## Design Philosophy
All four classes must be equally viable for the entire game. The design principle: every dungeon, every floor, has content specifically requiring each class's strengths. A solo player of any class can complete the game — but they'll need to improvise workarounds for other-class content. A party covering all four classes has the optimal experience.

No class is the "damage dealer" or the "support" exclusively. Each can fight. Each can explore. Each has unique puzzle access.

---

## CLASS 1: FIGHTER

**Flavor:** You went in with a sword. You'll come out with one — or not at all. The Fighters who survive are not the strongest. They're the ones who learned that strength alone doesn't open every door.

**Starting Stats:**
| Stat | Value | Modifier |
|------|-------|----------|
| STR | 16 | +3 |
| DEX | 10 | +0 |
| CON | 15 | +2 |
| INT | 8 | -1 |
| WIS | 12 | +1 |
| CHA | 9 | -1 |

**Starting HP:** 12 (d10 max + CON mod +2)
**Starting AC:** 16 (Chain Mail base 15 + no DEX bonus — too slow)
**Starting Equipment:** Long Sword (d8 slashing), Chain Mail, Shield, 3 Torches, 5 Bread, 2 Water Flasks
**Proficiencies:** All weapons, heavy armor, shields

**Level Up HP:** d10 + CON modifier per level

**UNIQUE ABILITIES:**

**1. Shield Bash (Active, Combat)**
- Once per combat, as an action: Slam shield into one adjacent enemy.
- Deals d6 bludgeoning damage.
- Target makes STR save (DC = 8 + STR mod + proficiency = DC 13 at level 1).
- Fail: Stunned 1 turn (enemy loses next action entirely).
- Success: Pushed 1 tile back.
- Level 3 upgrade: Can target two adjacent enemies simultaneously.

**2. War Cry (Active, Combat)**
- Once per floor: Shout to draw all enemy attention.
- For 3 turns: All enemies in the room target the Fighter only (not party members).
- Fighter gains +2 AC during this duration.
- Companion healing and repositioning can happen freely during these turns.
- Level 5 upgrade: Duration extends to 5 turns, +3 AC instead.

**3. Brute Force (Passive + Active, Exploration)**
- Passive: Can attempt to force open stuck/locked doors. Roll d20 + STR mod vs DC 15. On success: door opens, no key needed (destroys the door — cannot be used for stealth). On fail: door stays closed, 1 turn wasted.
- Active: Can push/lift stone blocks and debris. Opens Fighter-specific paths in every dungeon.
- Specific interactions: Break weakened walls (marked with crack texture), hold open closing gates (STR contest vs gate mechanism DC 14, grants party passage), bend iron bars (cells, cages).
- Level 4 upgrade: Brute Force succeed DC drops to 12. Can now break some locked chests directly.

**Playstyle Description:**
The Fighter is not a blunt instrument. They're a problem-solver who uses STR where others use keys. Every dungeon has at least 3 Fighter-specific interactions per floor. Players who learn to look for cracked walls and bent bars will find shortcuts no other class can access. In combat: front-line. Absorbs damage. Shield Bash at the right moment can save the party from a devastating enemy turn. War Cry is a lifesaving ability for parties in trouble — it draws fire while the Mage sets up a killing combo or the Cleric heals.

**Class-Specific Loot:**
- Greatsword (d10, two-handed, Fighter only, requires dropping shield)
- Full Plate (AC 18, Fighter only — heaviest armor in the game)
- Champion's Shield (+3 AC on Defend action instead of +2)

---

## CLASS 2: RANGER

**Flavor:** The dungeon is a map. You read it before you enter, as you walk it, and you keep reading it even after the torch goes out. Other adventurers see darkness. You see patterns.

**Starting Stats:**
| Stat | Value | Modifier |
|------|-------|----------|
| STR | 10 | +0 |
| DEX | 16 | +3 |
| CON | 12 | +1 |
| INT | 10 | +0 |
| WIS | 14 | +2 |
| CHA | 8 | -1 |

**Starting HP:** 9 (d8 max + CON mod +1)
**Starting AC:** 14 (Studded Leather 13 + DEX mod +1 capped at +2)
**Starting Equipment:** Long Bow (d8 piercing), Short Sword (d6 slashing), Studded Leather, 20 Arrows, 3 Torches, 4 Bread, 2 Water Flasks, 1 Snare Trap
**Proficiencies:** All ranged weapons, light and medium armor, martial melee weapons

**Level Up HP:** d8 + CON modifier per level

**UNIQUE ABILITIES:**

**1. Eagle Eye (Active, Exploration & Combat)**
- Once per room (resets on room entry): Reveal one tile in any direction beyond the torch radius.
- Shows: Enemies (with HP bar), traps (highlighted in red), loot (highlighted in gold), walls, NPCs.
- In combat: Can use to spot incoming enemies before they act (gives +1 to initiative against them for that round).
- Level 3 upgrade: Reveals 3 tiles in a cone instead of 1 tile in a direction.

**2. Snare Trap (Active, Combat & Exploration)**
- Place a snare on any tile within 2 tiles (5-second setup time — vulnerable during this).
- Enemy stepping on snare: Restrained for 2 turns (cannot move, -2 AC, attacks against them have advantage).
- Can hold 3 active snares at once (consume snare materials from inventory to craft more — 1 Rope + 1 Iron Spike = 1 Snare).
- Level 4 upgrade: Snares now also deal d6 piercing damage on trigger.

**3. Forager (Passive, Exploration)**
- Passive: When searching barrels, crates, or rooms (Search action), Ranger has a 30% chance to find food or water that other classes would miss.
- Passive: Ranger can identify tracks on the floor (enemies passed this way, N turns ago, direction of travel). Information displayed in text above floor texture.
- Active: Ranger can forage in dungeon rooms with organic material (plants, mold, mushrooms) — 50% chance per attempt to produce 1-2 food units. Attempt takes 1 turn.
- Level 5 upgrade: Forager now also has 20% chance to find rare herbs (crafting ingredient for high-tier potions).

**Playstyle Description:**
The Ranger is the dungeon's reader. Where Fighters see walls, Rangers see weak points. Where others see a pit trap, the Ranger highlights it from 2 tiles away. In combat: stay in the back row, fire arrows with DEX accuracy, place snares before the Fighter engages. The Ranger's Eagle Eye ability means that if played well, you almost never get surprised. Trap spotting alone makes Dungeon 6 (The Shattered Halls) far more survivable. The Ranger is also the survival specialist in dungeons where food and water are scarce — Forager makes longer runs viable in Dungeons 4 and 5 where resources are stretched.

**Class-Specific Loot:**
- Compound Bow (d10, requires 2 turns to fire but cannot miss on a 10+ roll)
- Shadow Quiver (arrows never run out — replenishes 5 arrows per floor at no cost)
- Tracker's Boots (immune to floor sliding effects, no sound when moving)

---

## CLASS 3: MAGE

**Flavor:** Every wall is a door if you understand what it's made of. Every enemy is a puzzle if you know what element breaks it. The torch you carry doesn't matter. The fire you make does.

**Starting Stats:**
| Stat | Value | Modifier |
|------|-------|----------|
| STR | 8 | -1 |
| DEX | 12 | +1 |
| CON | 10 | +0 |
| INT | 17 | +3 |
| WIS | 10 | +0 |
| CHA | 13 | +1 |

**Starting HP:** 4 (d4 max + CON mod 0)
**Starting AC:** 11 (Padded 11 — that's it. No DEX bonus added to Padded in heavy use)
**Starting Mana:** 20 (INT mod +3 = 23 — base mana pool 10 + INT mod × 3 + level bonus)
**Starting Equipment:** Staff (d6 bludgeoning, spell focus), Padded Armor, Spell Codex (shows discovered combos), 3 Spell Scrolls (one each: Fire Bolt, Ice Shard, Wind Slash), 2 Torches, 3 Bread, 3 Water Flasks
**Proficiencies:** Light armor only, staves and daggers, all spell schools

**Mana Cost by Spell:**
| Spell | Cost | Dice | Element |
|-------|------|------|---------|
| Fire Bolt | 4 | d8 | Fire |
| Ice Shard | 4 | d6 | Ice |
| Stone Fist | 5 | d10 | Stone |
| Wind Slash | 4 | d6 | Wind |
| Shadow Strike | 5 | d8 | Shadow |
| Holy Light | 5 | d6 (d10 vs undead) | Light |
| Spark (light) | 1 | — | Fire utility |
| Darkvision | 8 | — | Shadow utility |

**Level Up HP:** d4 + CON modifier per level
**Level Up Mana:** +5 per level + INT modifier

**UNIQUE ABILITIES:**

**1. Spell Combination (Active, Signature Ability)**
- Combine any two element spells into an advanced effect.
- Cost: Sum of both spells' mana costs.
- Known combinations (discovered through experimentation or wall markings):
  - Fire + Wind = Inferno Gust: d10 fire damage, blast doors open, clears gas clouds
  - Ice + Stone = Frost Lock: freeze mechanism in place (disarm traps, lock doors shut on enemies), deals d8 ice
  - Shadow + Light = Reveal: expose hidden passages, invisible enemies, true nature of illusions (no damage)
  - Fire + Stone = Magma Seal: forge a bridge of cooled lava over gaps, seal doors shut (d8 fire)
  - Wind + Ice = Blizzard: AOE d6 cold to all enemies in room, slow all for 2 turns (DEX save DC 14)
  - Shadow + Wind = Phantom Step: pass through thin walls (1 tile thick max), duration 5 seconds
  - Light + Stone = Sunlance: piercing light beam through multiple targets in a line, d12 light
  - Ice + Fire = Steam Cloud: blind all in room 2 turns (CON save DC 13), d4 heat to all, CON save DC 12 or avoid
- Unknown combinations exist — discovered only through play.
- Level 3: Unlock one additional random combination per level.

**2. Mana Surge (Active, Combat — Once per Rest)**
- Declare Mana Surge before casting any spell.
- Spell deals double damage dice.
- Costs double mana.
- If mana is insufficient after: drawn from HP at 2:1 ratio (2 HP = 1 mana).
- At 0 HP: Surge fails, unconscious.
- Level 5: Mana Surge now triples damage dice (from double) — at high risk.

**3. Arcane Decipherment (Passive + Active, Exploration)**
- Passive: Mage automatically reads and understands runes, ancient text, spell formulas on walls. Other classes must make INT check DC 14 or wait for the Mage.
- Active: Can analyze any magic item to reveal its properties (takes 1 turn — replaces Identify scroll for Mage).
- Specific interactions: Decode magic door passwords, understand Aureate Circle ward logic (Dungeon 9), translate Dorevus's wall writings (Dungeon 10).
- Level 4 upgrade: Can now sense hidden magic (like Detect Magic — highlights magical objects/traps/doors in a blue glow).

**Playstyle Description:**
The Mage is a glass cannon with a unique risk calculus. Low HP and AC means constant positioning decisions — stay out of melee at all costs. In a party: back row, use spells with elemental precision (check the bestiary, hit weaknesses). Solo: Darkvision negates the torch resource entirely (at mana cost), Shadow Phase from the Fragment opens alternate routes, Spell Combination solves puzzles no other class can brute-force. The most skill-expressive class — a skilled Mage player who knows every combination and every elemental weakness will feel the most powerful late-game. An unskilled Mage dies on Floor 1.

**Class-Specific Loot:**
- Archmage Robes (AC 13 — best mage armor in game — +2 INT)
- Infinity Focus (reduces all spell costs by 2 mana, staff only)
- Tome of Combinations (reveals 3 unknown combinations immediately — found only in Dungeons 8 and 9)

## CLASS 4: CLERIC

**Flavor:** Every dungeon has a wound. You are the one who decides which wounds matter. Faith is not hope. It is a tool. You use it precisely.

**Starting Stats:**
| Stat | Value | Modifier |
|------|-------|----------|
| STR | 12 | +1 |
| DEX | 10 | +0 |
| CON | 13 | +1 |
| INT | 10 | +0 |
| WIS | 16 | +3 |
| CHA | 14 | +2 |

**Starting HP:** 7 (d6 max + CON mod +1)
**Starting AC:** 15 (Chain Mail 15 — Clerics don't use shields typically but can)
**Starting Divine Power:** 18 (WIS mod +3 = base pool 10 + WIS mod × 3 — depletes like mana)
**Starting Equipment:** Mace (d6 bludgeoning, +2 vs undead), Chain Mail, Holy Symbol (focus for divine spells), 2 Heal Potions, 3 Torches, 4 Bread, 3 Water Flasks
**Proficiencies:** Light and medium armor, shields, maces, staves, clubs, all divine spells

**Divine Spell Costs:**
| Spell | Cost | Effect |
|-------|------|--------|
| Cure Wounds | 5 | Heal d8 + WIS mod HP |
| Mass Cure | 10 | Heal d6 + WIS mod to all party |
| Purify | 6 | Remove any status: poison, curse, blind, stun |
| Sense Evil | 2 | Detect undead/demons through walls up to 3 tiles |
| Holy Light | 5 | d6 light damage (d10 vs undead) |
| Bless | 4 | One ally: +1 to all attack rolls and saves for 3 turns |
| Holy Shield | 8 | Absorb next hit for one ally (any damage) |
| Turn Undead | 7 | All undead in room must WIS save DC 14 or flee 2 turns |

**Level Up HP:** d6 + CON modifier per level
**Level Up Divine Power:** +4 per level + WIS modifier

**UNIQUE ABILITIES:**

**1. Purify (Active, Exploration & Combat)**
- Can cleanse any corrupted object, door, or mechanism.
- In combat: Remove any status effect from any ally (see cost above).
- Exploration: Purify a corrupted shrine (converts it to a working shrine that gives buffs). Purify a poisoned water source (consumable, then safe to drink). Purify cursed items (remove curse — allows safe equipping of cursed gear that has good stats).
- Dungeon 9 specific: Reduces Judgment gain by 20% (the Sanctum recognizes divine affinity).
- Level 3: Purify now also restores 5 HP to the target in addition to status removal.

**2. Holy Shield (Active, Combat)**
- Target one ally.
- Next single hit against that ally is absorbed completely — no damage, no status.
- Includes hits that would be instant-kill (Rift Walker's auto-hit, Void Titan's Timeline Split).
- Duration: Until next hit or 3 turns (whichever comes first).
- Does not stack — cannot have two shields active on one target.
- Level 4 upgrade: Holy Shield now absorbs two hits before breaking.

**3. Sense Evil (Passive + Active, Exploration)**
- Passive: Cleric always senses undead and demonic enemies within 3 tiles (shown as a heartbeat sound cue + red glow at tile edge — does not reveal exact position, just direction).
- Active: Spend 2 Divine Power — reveals exact location and HP of all undead in current room for 1 turn.
- Dungeon-specific: In Dungeon 2 (Goblin Warrens), Sense Evil detects the Shaman before others can see them. In Dungeon 5 (Frozen Abyss), detects frozen undead prisoners before thawing — prevents ambushes.
- Level 5: Sense Evil extends to demons and cursed constructs, not just undead.

**Playstyle Description:**
The Cleric is the party's survival system. In a party: Holy Shield on the Fighter before a boss fight, Purify curse removal, Mass Cure in emergencies. Solo: Clerics have the highest survival rate in the early dungeons because their WIS saves resist most status effects, Sense Evil prevents ambushes, and self-healing keeps them going. The mid-game challenge: Divine Power management. Clerics who blow all their power on healing early arrive at bosses empty. Good Cleric play is resource conservation — knowing when to use a potion (free) vs a heal spell (costs power). The Cleric is uniquely important in Dungeon 9 (Crystal Sanctum) — the Judgment mechanic is forgiving to divine classes, and Turn Undead is the most efficient crowd control in The Final Descent.

**Class-Specific Loot:**
- War Hammer of Dawn (d8+2 bludgeoning, deals d10 light damage on top — dual damage on every undead hit)
- Aureate Vestments (Chain Mail equivalent AC, reduces all Divine Power spell costs by 2)
- Relic of Mending (once per floor, automatically activates when HP drops to 0 — triggers Cure Wounds, WIS modifier HP restored)

# SECTION 4: ITEM ECONOMY

---

## 4.1 ITEM TIERS

**Tier 1 — Common (Dungeons 1-3):**
Base weapons, basic armor, standard consumables. No enchantments. Low gold value.

**Tier 2 — Uncommon (Dungeons 3-6):**
Slight stat bonuses (+1 to relevant stat), minor elemental properties (fire arrows, frost daggers). Worth 3-5x Common value.

**Tier 3 — Rare (Dungeons 5-8):**
Named items with unique properties. Two-property items (AC+something, damage+status). Worth 10-20x Common value.

**Tier 4 — Legendary (Dungeons 8-10):**
Unique items with signature effects. Not craftable. One per dungeon maximum. Character-defining.

## 5.2 ENEMY SCALING MODEL

**HP Scaling:** Each dungeon tier adds roughly 50-75% HP to the base enemy types.
- Example: A Shadow Lurker in Dungeon 1 has 28 HP. A "Corrupted Lurker" variant in Dungeon 8 has 50 HP.
- Dungeon 10 variants: All returning enemies at +75% HP from their original values.

**AC Scaling:** Increases by 1 per two dungeon tiers.
- Dungeon 1-2: AC 10-14 range
- Dungeon 3-4: AC 12-16 range
- Dungeon 5-6: AC 14-18 range
- Dungeon 7-8: AC 15-19 range
- Dungeon 9-10: AC 16-20 range

**Attack Scaling:** +1 to hit and +1 base damage per dungeon tier pair.

**Enemy Behavior Complexity (escalating):**
- Tier I: Move toward player, attack. Basic aggression.
- Tier II: Use special ability once per combat. Some retreat at low HP.
- Tier III: Use abilities strategically (save stuns for fighters, use debuffs before attacks). Some have positioning preferences.
- Tier IV: React to player behavior (Dungeon 10's Echo Chambers — counter the player's primary strategy).

---

## 5.3 ELITE ENEMY RULES

Elite enemies appear once per floor as a mini-challenge encounter:
- HP is 2x the standard enemy of their type
- Always have one additional special ability
- Guaranteed uncommon or rare loot drop
- Marked with a gold border on their sprite when visible
- Do NOT respawn if killed in a run (floor remains easier for rest of run)

---

## 5.4 MINI-BOSS VS BOSS DISTINCTION

**Mini-Boss:**
- Guards an important item or floor transition
- Has unique model (larger sprite or different color palette)
- Guaranteed to drop a key, a named item, or a dungeon-specific resource
- Does not have a multi-phase combat
- Appears on Floor 1 or 2, never the final floor of a dungeon

**Boss:**
- Appears on the deepest floor of each dungeon
- Multi-phase combat (at least 2 phases — Phase 1: standard combat, Phase 2: mechanic shift)
- Environment interaction during combat (lava pools, flooding, spawning enemies, etc.)
- Always drops the dungeon's Sunstone Fragment
- Always drops a named Tier 3 or 4 item
- Unique audio cue when combat begins (boss theme layer activates in the Web Audio API)

# SECTION 6: META-PROGRESSION SYSTEM

---

## 6.1 WHAT PERSISTS (localStorage)

**Always Persists — Never Lost:**
- Sunstone Fragment collection (which of 10 are found)
- Bestiary entries (every enemy encountered, weaknesses discovered)
- Spell Codex (every combination discovered)
- Reputation Title and total points
- Trophy Wall records (deepest floor, enemies slain, gold earned all-time)
- NPC relationship levels (Aldric, Mira, Orin — tracked as 0-10 trust scores)
- Tavern Upgrade unlocks

**Persists Per Run — Resets on Death:**
- Character level and XP
- Equipped items and inventory
- Gold on hand
- Current dungeon progress
- Food and water bars

**Gold and Materials — Hybrid Model:**
50% of gold carried when dying is lost. 50% is recovered (Aldric "kept it safe"). Materials picked up from completed dungeons persist permanently (you brought them out before the fatal run). This prevents total loss and maintains pressure without being punishing.

---

## 6.2 TAVERN UPGRADES

The Rusty Flagon expands as the player progresses. Gold spent at the tavern for permanent upgrades.

**Tier 1 Upgrades (available from run 1):**
- **Extended Bar** (100 gold) — Bessa's inventory adds 3 additional item slots. She restocks daily.
- **Map Room** (150 gold) — Notice board adds a 4th dungeon option each day (normally 3).
- **Stable** (80 gold) — Overworld path traversal cut from 60 to 30 seconds. Permanent.

**Tier 2 Upgrades (requires 2 fragments found):**
- **Alchemist's Corner** (250 gold) — Unlocks crafting at the tavern. Required for all recipes.
- **Trophy Alcove Expansion** (200 gold) — Trophy wall shows kill counts per enemy type, not just skulls.
- **Orin's War Table** (180 gold) — Orin posts enemy intel hints daily (one free bestiary entry revealed per day on a rotation).

**Tier 3 Upgrades (requires 5 fragments found):**
- **Mira's Cartography Room** (350 gold) — Partial dungeon maps available for purchase. 40% of dungeon pre-revealed on dungeon entry.
- **The Back Room** (400 gold) — Aldric reveals his history. Unlocks extended dialogue + gives permanent "Aldric's Advice" (+1 to all saving throws on the first floor of any dungeon).
- **Ghost Registry** (300 gold) — Wall in the tavern tracks every player character death, with floor and cause. Shows the full run history visually.

**Tier 4 Upgrades (requires 8 fragments found):**
- **The Vault** (500 gold) — Safe storage. Up to 10 items persist between runs (not lost on death — held in the Vault). Changes the risk calculus for legendary items.
- **Elden's Table** (0 gold — requires Elden event trigger) — Elden becomes permanent, appears every session. Gives one cryptic tactical hint per dungeon type per day.

---

## 6.3 NARRATIVE THROUGH DEATH — HADES MODEL (DETAILED)

The mechanism: every player death creates a **Death Echo**. The Death Echo stores:
- Which dungeon (1-10)
- Which floor (1-5)
- What killed them (enemy name)
- Character class
- Fragments held at time of death
- Run number (how many total runs)

**Death Echo Playback:**
On next tavern entry after a death, NPCs react:

**Aldric (1-3 runs):** "Back then. What got you?"
**Aldric (4-10 runs):** "That's the third time that floor's sent you back. You learning its rhythm yet?"
**Aldric (10+ runs):** "How many times is it now? You keep getting up. That matters."

**Mira:** Uses the death location to update her map. After 3 deaths on the same floor: "I've marked where you keep dying. There's something I didn't draw on the first map. Something I couldn't explain." New map detail appears — may be the answer to what's killing the player (wall marking location, trap square).

**Orin:** References death by enemy if it's an enemy he knows about. "The War Goblin on Level 2? Yeah. I heard that guy. Listen — his left side is open when he winds up. You can get him."

**Elden (Table Seven):** Most reflective. Death commentary is the most mechanically dense. "The Frost Wraith got you because you weren't watching the initiative order. It moves before you if you rolled below 13. Every time." This is the Hades-style revelation-through-death: Elden tells you what you did wrong in the previous run in concrete mechanical terms.

**Ghost System:**
At 5+ runs, dead characters begin appearing as ghost NPCs in the dungeons where they died:
- Ghost is translucent, standing on the tile where they fell
- Can interact with them: they say one thing (voice from the last action log of their death — "I was going for the key in the chest when the Mimic—")
- If the run succeeds past that ghost's death point: the ghost's name appears on the trophy wall with a small star ("Avenged")

**The Legend Layer (Strider Effect — 10+ runs):**
After 10+ runs, the tavern begins to whisper. Translucent text fades in at screen edges, referencing the player's accumulated stats:
- "...they say one walks among us who has died forty-three times and still returns..."
- "...the Fragment of Dawn sits on the trophy wall, and they say there are six more just like it..."
- Other players' anonymous run data can be seeded here in the multiplayer layer.

---

## 6.4 KNOWLEDGE AS PROGRESSION (THE ROGUELIKE CORE)

The meta-progression system is designed so that knowledge is the most powerful persistent asset:

**Bestiary completion:** Knowing that Rime Lichs drain STR (not just deal damage) means bringing Restore items. Not knowing means two STR drains and you're fighting at -4 to melee.

**Spell Codex completion:** Knowing Shadow+Light bypasses the Archivist of Nothing's Phase 2 means the fight is manageable. Not knowing means the fight is nearly impossible.

**NPC trust levels:** At trust level 8 with Orin, he gives the sibling quest — but also tells you about a specific hiding spot on Dungeon 2's Floor 2 that has a Greater Potion. This is information only. No UI flag. The player must remember it.

**Dungeon layouts change per seed daily — but the puzzles repeat.** The valve sequence in Dungeon 3 changes daily (different order), but the mechanic is always the same. Players who understand the mechanic solve it in 30 seconds. Players who don't flood themselves every time.

This is intentional: the game gets easier not because the player's character is stronger, but because the player is smarter.

# SECTION 7: DAILY & SOCIAL SYSTEMS

---

## 7.1 DAILY SEEDED RUN SYSTEM

**Seed generation:** A daily seed is generated server-side at midnight UTC. The seed is a string: `DUNGEON-X-{YYYY}-{MM}-{DD}`. Used as input to a seeded PRNG (mulberry32 or similar).

The seed controls:
- Which 3 dungeons appear on the notice board
- Enemy positions per dungeon per floor
- Loot positions and contents
- Puzzle solution order (valve sequences, mechanism orders, etc.)
- NPC dialogue variant pool selection
- Wandering NPC positions on the overworld path

**Seeded run scoring:**
Players who complete a seeded dungeon are scored:
- Base score: Floors completed × 1000
- Gold found (full amount × 10 points)
- Enemies killed (50 pts each)
- Bonus: No deaths (+500)
- Bonus: All party members alive (+300)
- Bonus: Under 10 minutes (+200)
- Penalty: Each party member death (-100)

**Leaderboard reset:** Daily at midnight UTC. Top 10 stored permanently in Hall of Fame (localStorage + optional server). Players can see:
- Daily leaderboard (today's seed)
- All-time Hall of Fame (best 3 runs ever)
- Personal best per dungeon

---

## 7.2 DEATH MARKERS — GHOST DATA SHARING

**Implementation:** When a player dies in a seeded daily run, their death position is logged:
```
{seed: "DUNGEON-X-2026-03-09", dungeon: 3, floor: 2, tile: {x:4, y:6}, cause: "Iron Golem Sentry", class: "Ranger"}
```

If a server is connected: death data is anonymized and shared. On other players' runs in the same seeded dungeon, a faint red glow marks tiles where others died. Hovering/touching the tile shows: "Adventurer fell here. Cause: Unknown." (never reveals class or specifics — just the existence of death at that location).

**Without server:** Death markers are local only — showing where the same player died on previous runs of the same dungeon seed.

**Value:** This is not cosmetic. Death markers are genuine tactical information. A tile with three red glows means three people died there. That's a trap, a Mimic, or a particularly hard enemy encounter — worth slowing down before entering.

---

## 7.3 COMMUNITY MESSAGE BOARD

**Location:** In the tavern, beside the notice board.

**Mechanism:** After any completed run (survive to exit a dungeon), player is offered an optional message board submission. 60-character limit. One submission per completed seeded run per day.

**Display:** Other players' messages appear on the message board in the tavern, attributed to the dungeon they completed: "From the Whispering Crypts: 'Fire opens the sealed door. Do not use ice.'"

**Moderation model:** Server-side basic profanity filter. Community flagging. The message board is asynchronous ambient social — not a chat. Atmosphere, not communication.

**Without server:** Board shows previous personal run messages only — the player's own notes from past runs.

---

## 7.4 GUILD SYSTEM

**Name:** Adventurer's Guilds (the in-world term) — "Guild" in the UI.

**Formation:** Any player can create a guild (requires 5 completed dungeon runs minimum). Guild code (6-character string) shared out-of-game to invite others.

**Guild size:** 2-10 members.

**Guild Objectives (refresh weekly):**
Three shared objectives with a difficulty tier:
- Easy: "Guild collectively earns 5,000 gold from seeded runs this week"
- Medium: "Guild defeats the boss of Dungeon 4 collectively 10 times this week"
- Hard: "A guild member completes Dungeons 1-5 in a single session"

**Guild Rewards (completion):**
- Easy: 100 gold per member + Emblem cosmetic (small guild badge on leaderboard name)
- Medium: 300 gold per member + Guild Trophy displayed in tavern
- Hard: Legendary cosmetic (unique player name color on leaderboard) + 500 gold per member

**Guild Leaderboard:** Top 10 guilds ranked by weekly combined score (member scores summed). Resets weekly.

**Implementation priority:** Guild server sync is Phase 3-4 feature. Local co-op guild tracking (manual score entry) can be Phase 1 social layer.

---

## 7.5 RIVAL SYSTEM

**Mechanism:** The game matchmakes the player against a "Rival" — another player of similar skill level (based on best run score within 20% of current player's best).

**Rival display:** A ghost run of the Rival's best seeded dungeon run is visible in the dungeon. The Rival's position (where they were at the same point in the run) is shown as a faint outline character. The player can see if they're ahead or behind the Rival in real-time.

**Rival notifications:** After completing a run: "You beat [Rival]'s best time by 43 seconds." Or: "[Rival] just set a new personal best. They're ahead again."

**Rival changes:** Weekly. If you beat your Rival's score consistently, you're promoted to a faster Rival.

function mulberry32(seed) {
  return function() {
    seed |= 0; seed = seed + 0x6D2B79F5 | 0;
    var t = Math.imul(seed ^ seed >>> 15, 1 | seed);
    t = t + Math.imul(t ^ t >>> 7, 61 | t) ^ t;

  }
}

function attackRoll(attackerMod, targetAC) {
  const roll = Math.floor(Math.random() * 20) + 1;
  if (roll === 1) return { hit: false, critical: 'fumble', roll };
  if (roll === 20) return { hit: true, critical: 'crit', roll };
  return { hit: (roll + attackerMod) >= targetAC, critical: null, roll };
}

// Damage dice parser
function rollDamage(notation) {
  const match = notation.match(/(\d+)d(\d+)(?:[+](\d+))?/);
  if (!match) return 0;
  const [, count, sides, bonus] = match;
  let total = parseInt(bonus || 0);
  for (let i = 0; i < parseInt(count); i++) total += Math.floor(Math.random() * parseInt(sides)) + 1;
  return total;
}
